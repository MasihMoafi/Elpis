Elpis's pressure trigger is documented at 30%, yet the line above tops out at 29.1% — that is not a contradiction, because the trigger does not read the same quantity this chart plots. pressure_reached() (context_pruner.rs:217-223) compares get_total_token_usage() against 30% of the context window. get_total_token_usage() (context_manager/history.rs:344-352) is the last completed request's last_token_usage.total_tokens — input and output and reasoning, not input alone — plus a local, approximate token count of any tool-call items added to history since that request (tokens_not_covered_by_last_usage, an estimate, not the provider's exact count). The chart plots only last_token_usage.input_tokens of each completed request: narrower than what the trigger reads, and it excludes not-yet-billed tool output accumulating mid-turn. The two are different measurements of "how full is the context," not the same number read twice.

7 — Conclusions

RQ1 — Context efficiency

On a matched 4-round workload, Elpis held live context far lower than native Codex: peak , median , mean . Codex's context grew essentially uncontrolled through round 2, reached 92.3% of the 258,400-token window, and triggered its native compactor exactly once, mid-round-4. Elpis's pressure trigger fired 8 times and its steady trigger 3 times; its plotted live-context line never exceeded 29.1%, though its internal trigger reads a broader quantity than that plotted line (Section 2) and legitimately crosses its own 30% threshold at those 8 pressure firings. This is a matched comparison — same 4 rounds, same model, same prompts — and it shows Elpis's pruning engaging repeatedly and early, well before Codex's single, much larger native compaction.

RQ4 — Overhead vs. benefit

Elpis's 11 Ace passes, all run at maximal reasoning effort, cost tokens directly — about of Elpis's own total measured tokens, at Ace tokens spent per token reclaimed. Against that: Elpis's cumulative main-model input across the whole run was versus Codex's , driven almost entirely by round 2, where Codex's uncontrolled context growth multiplied the cost of every subsequent resend. Elpis's cache-hit share was lower ( vs. Codex's ) — the expected cost of rewriting context mid-session, since each rewrite invalidates the provider-side cache prefix. On wall-clock time, Elpis's 4 rounds took about 26.5% longer than Codex's 4 rounds summed. What Elpis bought: live context bounded under throughout, vs. Codex's uncontrolled climb to and a forced compaction; and full per-pass auditability (Section 5). Monetary cost of any of this: Unavailable — no authoritative pricing for the model used.

RQ5 — Auditability

All 11 Elpis maintenance passes are individually inspectable — trigger, exact Ace conversation, billed usage, and a per-tool-call kept/deleted decision with recoverable originals (Section 5), for both trigger types and with zero failures to compare against. What remains unverified: Ace's "kept" judgments at scale beyond the spot check performed, and any failure-path behavior, since none of the 11 passes failed in this run.

Limitations

This is one paired run, not a replicated study — no error bars, no statistical test, n=1 per arm. Codex triggered native compaction exactly once in this run; its behavior under repeated or more severe pressure is not observed. Token-reclaimed figures for Elpis are char-based estimates, not a re-tokenization. No monetary cost figures are reported anywhere in this document.
