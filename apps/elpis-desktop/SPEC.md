# Elpis desktop requirements

These are the required behaviors, numbered 1–85 without gaps; refer to one as "SPEC item N". The IDs in parentheses are the older request IDs that code, tests and REQUESTS.md still cite. [ES.md](../../ES.md) records current state and open decisions; [REQUESTS.md](../../REQUESTS.md) records requests. Project rules are in [MASIH-RULES.md](../../docs/MASIH-RULES.md), the design system in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), and verification in [TESTING.md](TESTING.md).

## Sidebar and chat list

1. Session Manager is removed; no background process archives chats on its own. (S18)
2. Pinned, Projects, Groups and Recents show chats the person started or placed. Recents excludes project chats. Workflows and background runs have separate, collapsible sections controlled by switches that default to off; search returns matching chats, including children, in one flat list. Hiding a chat does not archive or delete it. (S13, S18, OM1, OM2, R41)
3. Native subagents, chats created by a listed chat, workflow steps and manually arranged chats form one recursive tree. Each generation has one indentation step; only rows with children show a chevron. Branches start collapsed on reload and do not open merely because their chat opens. When the person archives a parent, its recorded `create_thread` children are archived with it. Load-more controls expose further children without losing their parents; orphaned chats remain visible. (S23, O1, C4, A154, R23, R45, R73)
4. A subtree can move under another chat, into a project or back to the main list. Moving a parent keeps its descendants. Reject cycles and self-drops; preserve the engine task and permissions. Save placement and order together, retain the selection on failure, and open a successful move's destination. (O1, O2, R73)
5. “Move to project…” and “New project from these…” work on every selected chat, including a running one. Creating a project asks for a name and a folder from a menu or chooser. Cancellation and failed saves leave chats unchanged. (O2)
6. Chats needing the person precede working chats. A newly finished unread chat follows the working chats, before idle chats. Status follows R8 and R44; user-assigned conversation colours follow R84 and remain distinct from status. Colours can be reset and survive reload, including for nested agents. (S22, C8, A153)
7. An unnamed chat the person started receives a task name after its first completed message: a read-only side turn uses the small naming model at low effort, or the chat model if unavailable. Save a title of at most 36 characters only if still unnamed; a manual rename cancels it, and scans or timers do not trigger naming. Background and workflow runs retain their parent and identity; opening one does not send a model request. (S18)
8. The person can pin, archive, rename, reorder and open chats; F2 renames the selected chat. Bookmark and ordering changes survive reload. Menus group organization and Open in actions, and singular or multi-selection labels match their targets; Show agents appears only when a chat has agents, and New Session in New Window is absent from chat menus. (C8)
9. The Love Spot, Projects, Pinned, Recents, Groups and Workflows sections fold; section and project folds survive restart. Workflows starts folded. The sidebar shows complete rows above its footer and remains usable at every window height. (S25, OM2, A159–A161)
10. Clean up finished machine and test chats permanently, along with their worktrees and branches, preserving real chats and unmerged work. Deleting a chat never deletes another; a chat with forks is archived instead. (S15)
11. Arrow keys open the reached chat or page and select the reached tab or radio. Search, New chat and switches receive focus without activation. Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+] move a chat to the top or bottom of its list; Ctrl+Alt+[ and Ctrl+Alt+] change split-view focus. (S22, R12)

## Chat, composer and turns

12. Sent input appears at once. Drafts survive navigation, reconnect and restart. Typing stays available while disconnected; runtime actions explain when a connection is required and are not replayed automatically. Messages queued during a turn remain in order. (S34, C5, R2, R3, R4, R66, R69)
13. The composer stays on one row at narrow widths. Thinking, effort, Send and Stop remain accessible; secondary controls move into the Plus menu. Fast applies at the next model request in the running turn, persists for later turns and shows the acknowledged state; unsupported routes explain why without starting a turn. (S32, S35, C5, R85, R87)
14. User and assistant messages render in full, without Collapse or Expand previews. Copy retains the complete message. Image replies identify the selected image and source message, show a thumbnail, focus the composer and send that image's bytes. Replacing or cancelling the reply preserves unrelated attachments and drafts; failed image reads show an error. (S image replies, R27)
15. Hide a Reasoning disclosure when no recorded or streamed summary exists. Preserve actual summaries, history and copying. A finished turn says “Worked for …” when its timing is known; otherwise it summarizes the recorded activity by kind. (S36, R88)
16. The prompt rail appears from the fourth prompt onward, as in the ChatGPT app. It stays beside the transcript without squeezing the chat or moving Working. The Working placement is owned by [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#5-layout). (C6, R24)
17. Message actions and activity rows keep the spacing in the design system. The final reply keeps its hanging actions; mid-turn replies offer them on right-click. Load earlier messages has its own row and a 24 px gap before the transcript; it preserves the reading position and offers retry on failure. (C6)
18. `/resume` opens a searchable saved-chat chooser even with the sidebar closed. Loading, empty, error and retry states are visible; opening a result does not send a turn. Escape and outside dismissal return focus. `/clear` works in the selected chat, and Select all acts on the focused editor or board. (C5)
19. The skills switch updates immediately and returns to its earlier state with a reason if the write fails. A displayed label is not an action. (S27, A165)

## Context, goals and files

20. `/goal <condition>`, `/goal` and `/goal clear` set, read and clear the selected chat's goal through its Claude or engine runtime. Refusals are visible and commands do not become user messages.
21. The Context panel shows the selected chat's goal with Clear, admitted files and project `ES.md`; files use the editable Files pane. A missing `ES.md` offers Create without replacing an existing file. Switching chats cannot display or change another chat's context; a rejected write preserves the existing state and offers retry. Context is read only while the panel is open.
22. `document_open` opens the requested existing text file in its own chat's Files pane, editable by default. It reports success after renderer acknowledgement; missing, binary, oversized and unavailable files return a clear error. (DOC1, DOC3)
23. `document_read` returns saved text, modification time and whether the editor has unsaved edits. Drafts survive reopening. Save and Ctrl+S make changes available to the agent; stale disk writes are refused without losing the draft. Another chat, window or website cannot acknowledge the request. (DOC2–DOC4, R3)
24. HTML and HTM files open in a rendered preview with bounded source access. Project-local classic CSS and JavaScript run in an opaque sandbox without changing project files. Unsupported resources are reported. (C10)
25. Text above the 512 KB editable limit remains readable in bounded pages up to the 30 MB preview cap. File cards and document selections offer editable feedback for the agent; sending stays explicit. HTML selections are accepted only from their own preview frame. (C10)
26. Paths and diff text wrap within their panel; line numbers and actions remain readable.
27. Empty Files uses a compact tree width. Opening a document restores the deliberate width or a readable default; closing the last file returns to the compact width. The divider remains resizable. (R18, R25)

## Browser and annotations

28. Each chat has its own browser. Its tabs, address, history, reload, stop and sidebar URL pins work without exposing the Elpis runtime to websites. Browser settings follow the ChatGPT desktop reference. (R10, R39)
29. Tab selection supports Shift ranges and grouped, icon-led actions. Close other tabs and Close tabs to the right affect the intended tabs; unselected tabs and deliberate panel sizes are preserved. An empty pane does not reopen as a wide blank view. (C9, R76)
30. Drawing uses the browser's own box over a still image; Add or Close restores the live page without moving the surrounding layout. Style opens and closes without changing marks or the selected tool. Chat pauses drawing and focuses the composer; Resume restores the marks and Discard is explicit. (S21, M1, M2, A151)
31. Enter sends a browser annotation once and in order. The sent message shows the person's comments, not the internal note for the model. Popup and dialog elements can be annotated without activating or dismissing them. (S17, C9, A147)
32. Shared web skills use bounded, standard interaction rules and give screenshots useful names. (C13)

## Love Spot and Matilda

33. Matilda is the default board collaborator. Writing, pasting or drawing on the board can receive a black handwritten reply with red hearts and her circular portrait beside it; the portrait also identifies her in the Love Spot chat, not ordinary chats. Preserve custom images. There is no separate Talk panel, Matilda button or agent-card section. Do not answer her own writing, a moved shape or an edit with unchanged words. (S20, S30, M3, A150, A162, R55)
34. A new board opens with a handwritten welcome and heart. Its default character can be moved and connected to things; Matilda's answer wait is configurable, with a four-second default. (S20, S30)
35. An open text editor, IME composition, paste or pause is unfinished input. Queued work rechecks editing and page identity; cancellation keeps pending text. Incoming replies never close the editor, steal focus or overwrite a draft. (C7, R75)
36. Matilda's editable professional persona and model choice persist and accompany her requests; she starts as a senior design collaborator, not a role-played character. An empty model choice uses the app default. Her reply and drawing stay together; supported fenced Excalidraw shapes are rendered rather than shown as raw data. The board shows a writing indicator while she answers. (S30)
37. Opening the board or its chat clears the Love Spot unread notice. A later reply can notify again. The board retains pages and artwork; its Style control can close and reopen without changing marks. Delete and its trash icon share a baseline in both themes and the standalone board. A page left with nothing on it is not kept unless marked Keep. In the Pages list, Up and Down open the page above or below the one shown; opening or deleting a page keeps the list open at its scroll position, with the shown page in view. A clicked screenshot thumbnail adds the screenshot to the open page instead of a new one. (H2, H3, C7, R70; 5 Nov)
38. Respect Masih's preference against cats on his board; removing a rejected painting preserves the surrounding conversation. (H1)
39. Board bars have no move handles; Excalidraw's GitHub, Follow, Discord and Library controls are absent. (S25)
40. The pinned companion floats over each app page, can be moved, pinned and unpinned, and keeps its position. Its mini chat preserves drafts and never changes the main composer. Clicking her opens a slim message field below her, as in ChatGPT Pets; it sends only on Enter or Send, and Esc, an outside click or another click on her closes it. It shows no earlier messages by default; one designated action reveals them. When Matilda is off, the pinned chat remains usable. (C7)
41. The companion's music control only mutes or unmutes the existing player and restores the prior volume. A drag does not activate the chat or move the content underneath it. A screenshot is taken once, only on request, through the system picker; cancelling returns no image. (C7)
42. Love Spot keeps text working and hides unavailable audio controls, including for legacy Love Spot chats. Unavailable controls never start speech or capture the microphone. Do not show a Matilda voice-call button. (C14, R71)

## Voice and read aloud

43. Dictation inserts editable text without sending it. Live voice, dictation and read aloud are distinct actions. Settings offers a saved call voice from the active connected protocol and advanced transcription options; unsupported voices are refused and a changed voice applies to the next call. A call has truthful connection state, microphone mute and End; ending it, or leaving an ordinary chat, releases microphone and peer resources. (V2–V4)
44. Where read aloud is available, finished visible assistant replies offer Read aloud and Stop, with reusable audio and speed control. Preserve words, numbers, code and order; remove only Markdown decoration. Playback does not require a microphone. (RA1–RA5, C14)
45. Read aloud uses the source selected in Models & accounts → Use for, defaulting to local Kokoro; live voice defaults to the connected ChatGPT subscription. A failed source never falls back to another provider. Unavailable capability is hidden or explained; unsupported quota and cost are not invented. (RA2, RA19, R71)
46. A shared speaker prevents overlapping playback across chats and windows. Stop or disconnect retires queued and late audio without stopping the text turn. Ordinary-chat audio ends on navigation; Matilda's voice and live chat continue across the app, with a floating control to stop her. Long replies play in order without losing or repeating chunks. (RA6–RA8, RA18)
47. Spoken requests become one turn in the selected chat through its provider and permissions, without starting or steering a separate Codex turn. Busy requests wait in order; each handoff and answer belongs to its originating chat. A failure preserves the full text, shows the reason and never blindly retries an uncertain delivery. (RA9–RA13, RA18)
48. Internal voice-handoff envelopes never appear as chat messages, recalled prompts or exports; ordinary messages that mention them stay visible. (V1)
49. The realtime speaker delegates questions, tool requests and capability checks to the same agent as typed chat, speaks its answer once and claims only verified account access. (V5, RA12–RA13)
50. When Matilda speech is available and unmuted, her board and chat replies appear as she writes and speech starts before completion, whichever text provider she uses. Old replies and commentary are not replayed and live-call audio is not duplicated. Board presses and new turns do not cut off her current speech. (RA14)
51. Streaming speech buffering and queueing are owned by [speech-buffer.mjs](bridge/speech-buffer.mjs) and [voice.mjs](bridge/voice.mjs). (RA15)
52. Speech excludes painting data, hidden reasoning and tools; streaming holds incomplete painting fences. One board note is updated and finalized once; cancelled text is not spoken. (RA16)
53. When available, one saved Mute control immediately stops Matilda's speech while writing continues. Unmuting speaks new text only; it does not replay old replies. (RA17)
54. Record time to first audible output, speech characters, session duration and available quota changes for cold and warm read aloud and Matilda speech, without storing words or credentials. (RA20)
55. Where a Love Spot live call is available, it uses the saved subscription voice, Matilda's persona and current board notes and shapes. Speech starts promptly, around 1.5 seconds after the person finishes, supports interruption and grows one note per reply; board edits update context and unseen results are never invented. (RA21, M4)

## Workflows

56. Workflows has its own page, navigation entry and sidebar group. A visible plain request and short pattern choices create or update a team without starting a run; More patterns offers the full catalogue. New starts with an empty team; Update uses the current team. Drafts and existing boards survive refusals and intervening edits; New checkpoints the old board for Undo. Offline edits stay available and runtime actions are not replayed on reconnect. (S12, C1, R72)
57. Keep the board focused: omit Kahn's order, waves, critical path, Father, auto-arrange, notes, the command bar, AutoCAD selection hints and the run planner.
58. Agents have a name, role, model, character and calm state: idle, working, needs you, done or failed. The six starter characters bring their role prompts and default to the main model. The person can edit an agent without losing its work or connections and open its last-run chat. Changing provider updates the model choices, including connected subscriptions; one connected agent can be added under another. (W1, W2, W4, C12, R14)
59. Connecting agents defines dependencies and passes each result to its downstream agents. Removing an agent removes its arrows; “Delete, keep the flow” is distinct from Delete. Selection follows R14 (item 58). A run starts only through an explicit action and explains why it cannot start; its chat shows the team's results. (W3, W5, W6, R20)
60. Runs use the chosen project folder or a private persistent workspace; report-only agents accept an empty project folder. Writers have bounded scopes and a dependent check. The Mother reviews the result before integration; conflicts and unavailable recovery are visible. See [WORK_GRAPHS.md](../../docs/WORK_GRAPHS.md) for the engine graph contract. (W7, C1–C3)
61. The board and its agents survive restart, including older saved boards. Saved workflows have unique names and colours; duplicate renames explain the refusal. A workflow has the same dot in the sidebar, header and Mother card. Each run appears once with its recorded step chats, expanded while running and folded when finished; legacy names use saved metadata without renaming engine chats. History is removable with recovery and closes on Escape, outside click or navigation, returning focus; test runs do not appear as user history. New workflow remains usable during a run. (W8, WI1, WI2, C1)
62. Chat, Flat and Isometric views show the same live Mother, team and recursively spawned descendants. Agent chats show their identity and recorded model, rendered messages and clickable tools. Saved routing and failures remain historical. Resume cannot duplicate active work; opening progress does not launch inference. (C2, C4)
63. Routing avoids known exhausted subscriptions. On a quota failure, recovery can use another connected subscription while preserving the run, completed work and worktrees; it does not retry while related agents are active. Unrelated failures are not blindly replayed. The Mother can make bounded, visible steering or retry decisions. (C3)
64. The chat `workflow` tool runs only for an explicit request by the person, even when Subagents is off; helper and autonomous turns cannot start it. It returns the run ID and saved script path without requiring the window to remain open. GPT and Claude chats use their own configured routes. (WR1, WR6)
65. The script language, safety bounds and worktree isolation are owned by [workflow-script.mjs](../../tools/elpis-claude/workflow-script.mjs) and [workflow-runner.mjs](../../tools/elpis-claude/workflow-runner.mjs). (WR2)
66. A workflow run persists its script, journal, agents and result. It resumes unfinished work after interruption, retries bounded failures, and can be stopped or resumed. An edited run reuses only its unchanged journaled prefix; the first changed call and later calls run again. (WR3–WR4)
67. A finished, stopped or failed run delivers one notification with its ID, status, result and script path to its originating chat when available. It is not a user prompt. An archived chat keeps the notice until restored; delivery does not unarchive it. (WR5)
68. Workflow-run RPCs, desktop routes and updates are owned by [client.mjs](bridge/client.mjs) and [server.mjs](bridge/server.mjs). (WR7)
69. The board and chat workflow tool use one runner: independent agents start together, dependents start after their inputs finish, and writers use isolated worktrees. (WR8)

## Permissions

70. Full access is the initial default in desktop and TUI for every provider and means no ordinary approval with full filesystem access; Bypass is a separate explicit choice. The saved default applies to chats without an explicit choice; a chat's selection survives reload. Retain identifiable older explicit choices; legacy fallbacks follow the default. Managed restrictions disable forbidden choices and reject them at the runtime boundary. (P1–P4, A233, A251)
71. Automatically started helpers operate within their declared scope and managed restrictions without asking the person for ordinary tool approval. A helper cannot grant itself a higher permission ceiling. (P3, R59, R60)
72. Changing a loaded helper's permissions or opening its live history uses its owning runtime. Unknown helpers and grants above the ceiling are refused. (`bridge/helper-desktop.test.mjs`)

## Settings, models and accounts

73. Settings navigation fits the available height without a scrollbar, keeps every destination reachable at smaller sizes and does not clip its title. Categories are plain group labels, not link-only overview pages. Shared rows keep consistent width, alignment and spacing; unsupported Flex is absent. (C11, R21, R67)
74. Known models and providers are selected from menus; custom model IDs are behind an explicit Advanced option. Provider order follows R35. The TUI and desktop use a live shared model catalogue, preserve the selection and show fetch failures. (S1, S2)
75. Features are grouped On then Off and alphabetized; the page identifies them as engine flags shared with the TUI. Skills have persistent, reversible switches that the engine honors. Plugins participate only after the person explicitly adds or enables them; the Claude and ChatGPT catalogues can be added deliberately. (S3, S9, S10)
76. Instruction and memory files are readable and editable. A missing file offers Create, a linked file edits its real target, broken links report an error and stale drafts are rejected. The interface states whether the model receives each source. Memories have one master switch and a saved-memory count; subordinate options appear under the master. (S4, S5)
77. Keyboard shortcuts are searchable, editable and resettable. A changed binding survives restart and appears beside the corresponding command; defaults include Ctrl+N for a new chat and Ctrl+, for Settings. (S6, S24, A155, R46)
78. Models & accounts exposes the connected providers and their own sign-in routes without returning credentials to the renderer. Claude accounts can be selected explicitly. MCP settings can import existing Claude Code connections without exposing keys or replacing existing connections. (C12)
79. Settings → Import chats lists Claude Code and Codex, with counts for interactive chats from the last 30 days; an empty source cannot be imported. Each press imports at most 50 without duplicates, reports skips, preserves originals and excludes subagent and exec runs. Codex rollouts up to 100 MB are copied and registered. Imported chats retain their names and can be opened and forked. (S31)
80. The Customize page is distinct from a chat's Context Ledger. Lab is absent from Settings and navigation. Personalization, model and account pages contain working controls rather than empty overview pages; the Personalization preview uses text without images. (S8, S28, S33)

## App shell and installation

81. The sidebar closes beside its brand and reopens from the title bar. The application exposes Back, Forward, File, Edit, View and Help. The footer shows the person's name as a label and places Theme beside Settings; the name is asked once and remains editable in Settings. (R67)
82. The startup screen uses the approved turning E without hexagons. Accepted artwork and the Working animation remain unchanged. (S26, A68)
83. A theme preview updates the native overlay and browser background without crashing. (H4)
84. Desktop interaction is fast and resource-efficient against upstream Codex, measured before and after changes; keep the [page shell](app/page.tsx) maintainable by splitting its responsibilities. (S16, A41, A47, A144, R9)
85. Installation follows C2 (item 60) and the desktop verification and preservation rules in [TESTING.md](TESTING.md). The candidate package opens successfully before the installed selector changes. (S29)
